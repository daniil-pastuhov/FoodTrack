# Community posts (drafts)

Drafts only: nothing here has been posted. Each one is written as the developer, says up front that
you made the app, and ends with one question. Post them yourself, one community at a time, and stay
to answer replies.

## Before posting anywhere

- [ ] Read that community's rules and pinned posts on self-promotion first, every time. Rules differ
      and change; nothing in this file says a post is allowed anywhere.
- [ ] If self-promotion is limited, use the place the community offers for it instead: a weekly
      self-promotion or "share your project" thread, a feedback or "rate my app" thread, a
      showcase channel, or ask the moderators first.
- [ ] Check what you have to say about yourself (some communities want a disclosure line, a flair
      or a tag).
- [ ] Post in a community you would also take part in otherwise; don't paste the same post into
      many places on the same day.
- [ ] Before the Recipe version is live, cut the Recipe paragraph or say "in the next update".
- [ ] Android: Google Play is in closed testing. Until it is public, say so, and don't post in
      Android-only places.
- [ ] Never ask for upvotes, ratings or reviews; ask for feedback.
- [ ] Menu sets (1 to 4 days, up to three courses a day) come from a paid recipe pack. Don't
      describe them as free. If you name a price, say "from USD 2.99 for 10 recipes, converted by
      the stores"; don't quote other currencies from memory.
- [ ] On-device recipes: Gemini Nano on Android writes English only for now, and Apple's model
      writes no Russian. Don't promise a free on-device recipe in a language it doesn't write.

Kinds of places to look at (check each one's rules): food-waste and zero-waste groups and forums,
meal-prep and home-cooking groups, budget and frugal-living groups, iOS and Android app
communities with a "share your app" thread, open-source and indie-developer communities, local
Norwegian groups about matsvinn, and Russian-speaking groups for people living in Norway or
elsewhere in Europe.

## 1. Zero-waste / food-waste community (English)

**Title:** I made a free app that reminds you before food goes off; your pantry stays on your phone

> Hi all. Disclosure first: I'm the developer, it's my own free app, and I'd like feedback from
> people who think about food waste more than most.
>
> BestBefore keeps track of what's in your fridge, freezer and cupboard. You scan a barcode (the
> product is filled in from Open Food Facts), point the camera at the printed date so the phone
> reads it, and you get a reminder before something expires. It shows what to use first, and
> essentials you use up go onto the shopping list.
>
> The new version adds a Recipe tab that takes whatever is closest to its date and writes a recipe
> from it. Two ways are free: share a ready-made prompt to an AI app you already use, or let a
> recent phone write it on the device. There are also optional paid recipe packs, one-off, no
> subscription, from USD 2.99 for 10 recipes (converted by the stores). Whatever a recipe needs
> and you don't have goes onto the shopping list in one tap.
>
> No account, no ads, no tracking, and it's open source. iPhone now; Android is in testing.
>
> My question: when food gets thrown away at your place, is it more often because you forgot it
> was there, or because you didn't know what to make with it?

## 3. iOS / Android apps community (English)

**Title:** I'm a solo dev in Norway: a free, open-source expiry tracker with on-device recipe generation

> Hi! I made BestBefore, a free food expiry tracker, as a solo developer. Two native apps, SwiftUI
> on iPhone and Jetpack Compose on Android, and the code is open source (Apache-2.0).
>
> What it does: scan a barcode (product from Open Food Facts), read the printed date with on-device
> text recognition, sort food by where you keep it, remind you before it expires, home screen
> widgets, a shopping list that fills from essentials you use up, and backup export. No account, no
> ads, no tracking (crash reports only); the pantry stays on the phone.
>
> New: a Recipe tab with three ways to get a recipe from what's expiring. Share a ready-made prompt
> to any AI app (free); run it on the device where the phone supports it, Apple Intelligence on
> iOS 26+ or Gemini Nano on some Android phones (free, nothing leaves the phone; English only on
> Android for now); or buy an optional pack of recipes written by the app's server, which can also
> plan menu sets of 1 to 4 days (one-off, no subscription, from USD 2.99 for 10 recipes).
> The app is in eight languages, and recipes can be written in eleven.
>
> iPhone: App Store. Android: in closed testing on Google Play.
>
> My question: if your phone can write the recipe on the device, would you still use the "share a
> prompt to your own AI app" option, or is that just clutter in the UI?

**Note for the owner, honestly:** in Russia, in-app purchases through the App Store and Google Play
are effectively unavailable at the moment, so readers there cannot buy recipe packs (the Russian

and the phone's own model, work there. The draft below does not promise packs; if you post in a
group aimed at people in Russia, leave the pack sentence out. Before posting, also check in App
Store Connect in which countries the app is offered.
