Larder

BestBefore should feel like a well-kept larder: oat paper, bottle green and a hearty book serif. Three rules guide every screen. Tell people what to use first in plain words. Keep colour for food that needs attention, so fresh food stays quiet. Treat a passed date as a fact to deal with, never a failure.

Colour primitives

Raw ramps. Screens never use these directly, only through the semantic roles below.

Semantic roles

What screens use, per theme. The expired, today and soon roles exist so food status can change without touching error colours.

Contrast (WCAG 2.2 AA)

Every pair the components use. Text needs 4.5:1, large text and UI parts 3:1. Generated by the build, so it can't drift from the tokens.

Typography

Vollkorn carries headings and covers Latin and Cyrillic. Body text uses the platform font: Roboto on Android, SF Pro on iOS. All sizes scale with the system text size.

Elevation

Only floating things get a shadow: Scan, snackbars, sheets and dialogs. Lists sit on the page with a hairline.

Icons

Lucide at 1.75 stroke. Category icons stand in when a product has no photo.

Illustrations

Only in empty states. One family: a thin line over soft shapes, no characters.

Expiry status

The app's most important component. Colour appears only when food needs attention. Every status has an icon and words, so it never depends on colour.

Past-date wording stays factual: "1 day over", never "Expired!" or "Wasted". Read aloud as "Sourdough bread, one day past its date".

Pantry row

Name, then status, then where it lives. Quantity sits at the end. Tapping opens the product. Rows stay one line each and truncate; the full name is on the detail sheet.

Row height at least 68dp. Swipe actions have a visible equivalent on the detail sheet, so nothing depends on a gesture.

Button

One primary button per view. Labels are verbs that say what happens: "Save to Fridge", not "OK".

Touch target at least 48dp on Android and 44pt on iOS. Loading keeps the button width so nothing jumps.

Text field

Always a visible label. Validate when the field loses focus.

Selection controls

Chips filter and pick one place. The round tick marks something done, and also appears on the shopping widget. Switches change a setting straight away.

Dialog, sheet, card

Dialogs are for decisions only. Sheets hold filters, sort and product details.

Empty state and loading

Pantry data is on the device, so lists open instantly with no skeletons. Loading indicators belong only to barcode lookup, receipts and image reads.

Error and feedback framework

Every message lands on exactly one surface. Walk these in order and stop at the first match.

  1. Must the person decide, or is it irreversible? T6 dialog.
  2. Tied to one input? T1 field error.
  3. Can the screen's content not be shown at all? T5 screen error.
  4. Does the condition last? T4 callout.
  5. Otherwise it's an event: T3 snackbar.

Reversible actions (use, toss or remove one item) happen straight away with Undo. Dialogs are for irreversible actions and real choices.

App icon direction

The icon moves from teal to bottle green: a preserving jar with a paper label, keeping the old icon's jar idea. Final artwork comes at implementation.
