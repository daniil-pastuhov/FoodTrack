Recipe

Turns what is about to expire into something to cook. The three soonest items are ticked already, a short form sets the kind of dish, and three routes write it: our server for a recipe from a pack, the device's own model for free where it has one, or a prompt handed to an AI app the person already uses. Each route is its own button and never falls back to another. A menu for three days is written by the server only. This tab replaces Calendar. Updated 14 Nov 2026 to match the apps: one action rule, How recipes work, Send all products, past-date warnings, and new Time, Meal and Diet controls.

Wording: the page says "this device", as Android does. iOS says "this iPhone" or "this iPad" in the same places (its {device} strings).

Recipe

Spinach and yoghurt eggs on sourdough toast

A warm, quick plate that uses the bread, the spinach and one of the yoghurts today.

15 min2 servingsEasy
Past its date: Sourdough bread (1 day over). Check it is still safe to eat before you cook.

Ingredients

  • 4 slicesSourdough breadIn your pantry
  • 100 gBaby spinachIn your pantry
  • 150 gGreek yoghurtIn your pantry
  • 4Free-range eggsIn your pantry
  • 1 cloveGarlic
  • 1 tbspOlive oil
  • Salt, pepper, chilli flakes

Method

  1. 1

    Stir the grated garlic and a pinch of salt into the yoghurt and leave it at room temperature.

  2. 2

    Toast the bread. Day-old sourdough toasts better than fresh.

  3. 3

    Warm the oil in a pan, add the spinach and turn it for a minute until it wilts. Season.

  4. 4

    Fry or poach the eggs to your liking.

  5. 5

    Spread the yoghurt on the toast, pile on the spinach, top with the eggs and finish with chilli flakes.

TipCheck the bread before using it: one day past a best-before date is usually fine, but not if there is any mould.

Spinach and yoghurt eggs on sourdough toast

A warm, quick plate that uses the bread, the spinach and one of the yoghurts today.

15 min2 servingsEasy
Past its date: Sourdough bread (1 day over). Check it is still safe to eat before you cook.

Ingredients

  • 4 slicesSourdough breadIn your pantry
  • 100 gBaby spinachIn your pantry
  • 150 gGreek yoghurtIn your pantry
  • 4Free-range eggsIn your pantry
  • 1 cloveGarlic
  • 1 tbspOlive oil
  • Salt, pepper, chilli flakes

Method

  1. 1

    Stir the grated garlic and a pinch of salt into the yoghurt and leave it at room temperature.

  2. 2

    Toast the bread. Day-old sourdough toasts better than fresh.

  3. 3

    Warm the oil in a pan, add the spinach and turn it for a minute until it wilts. Season.

  4. 4

    Fry or poach the eggs to your liking.

  5. 5

    Spread the yoghurt on the toast, pile on the spinach, top with the eggs and finish with chilli flakes.

TipCheck the bread before using it: one day past a best-before date is usually fine, but not if there is any mould.

Recipe

Slow-roasted salmon with spinach, yoghurt and sourdough crumb, served with a warm strawberry and black pepper salad

A longer cook for a weekend, using the salmon before Saturday along with the bread, the spinach, both yoghurts and the strawberries.

1 h 10 min4 servingsAmbitious
Past its date: Sourdough bread (1 day over). Check it is still safe to eat before you cook.

Ingredients

  • 2Salmon filletIn your pantry
  • 4 slicesSourdough bread, blitzed to coarse crumbsIn your pantry
  • 100 gBaby spinachIn your pantry
  • 300 gGreek yoghurtIn your pantry
  • 250 gStrawberriesIn your pantry
  • 1Unwaxed lemon, zest and juice
  • 2 clovesGarlic
  • 3 tbspOlive oil
  • 1 tspCoarsely ground black pepper
  • Flaky salt

Method

  1. 1

    Heat the oven to 120°C. Line a tray and bring the salmon out of the fridge twenty minutes before cooking so it cooks evenly.

  2. 2

    Toss the crumbs with two tablespoons of oil, the garlic and the lemon zest. Toast in a dry pan over a medium heat, stirring, until deep gold. This takes about eight minutes; do not walk away from it.

  3. 3

    Season the salmon, lay it skin down on the tray and roast for 25 to 30 minutes, until it flakes under gentle pressure but is still glossy in the middle.

  4. 4

    Halve the strawberries, warm them in a pan with the remaining oil and the pepper for two minutes, then add a squeeze of lemon.

  5. 5

    Fold the spinach through the yoghurt with a pinch of salt.

  6. 6

    Spoon the yoghurt on to plates, lay the salmon on top, scatter over the crumbs and serve the strawberries alongside.

TipLow oven temperatures vary. If the salmon still looks raw after 30 minutes, give it five more rather than turning the heat up.

How recipes work

Tick what you want to use up, choose the kind of dish, and get a recipe built around it. Recipes you get in the app are saved on this device as they arrive.

  • Free, with any AI app
    Share or copy a ready-made prompt to the AI app you already use.
  • Free, on this device
    This device writes the recipe itself, and nothing is sent anywhere.
  • From a recipe pack
    Our server writes the recipe, and can also plan a menu for 3 days. A recipe takes 1 recipe from a pack; a menu takes 4 recipes with 2 courses a day or 5 recipes with 3. Packs from £3.99. The free ways stay there when you have a pack.

You can open this again in Recipe settings.

The action rule

One rule decides the buttons, the same on both platforms (RecipeUiState.actions; iOS RecipeViewModel.actions). The primary is pinned above the pill; the rest sit under the form, in this order. Each is its own tap and never falls back to another, so a free route never spends a recipe.

Actions with a cost stay full width on phones; on tablets they take half the width, above the pill. The Share pill alone is as wide as its label and sits level with the pill on tablets, where it fits beside it.

Three routes, one form

The form and the ticked items feed all three routes. What differs is where the writing happens and what it costs. What a recipe and a menu take comes from our server with every count, so it can change without an app update; the apps show 1, 4 and 5 until they first hear from it.

The form controls

Time is a two-handle range slider (design system: Range slider) over total cooking time, 10 to 120 minutes in 5-minute steps. A handle at its end means no limit on that side, so the value reads "Any", "up to 30 min", "at least 40 min" or "20–45 min". Meal and Diet are multi-choice chips with a check on each chosen one: several meals mean any of them, several diets all apply. "Any" (meal) and "None" (diet) clear the others, and choosing one turns them off. In a menu the meal applies to the main courses only. The quick form on the tab shows Difficulty, Servings and Time; Meal and Diet live in Recipe settings and are summarised under More options. These controls are being built in the apps now: value and summary wording here is provisional until their strings land.

Ways in

The Recipe tab opens with the soonest items ticked. Get recipe ideas in Home's multi-select and the Recipe ideas action on a reminder open it with those items ticked instead. Neither writes anything by itself: the person still chooses a route. The first visit ever opens How recipes work.

When a recipe is taken

Only for something delivered, with one limit. A recipe or menu is held when Generate is tapped. If our side fails or times out, or the answer fails the quality check (no pantry item used, only staples, the same dish twice), it is always given back; after several technical failures in one day the server stops writing until the next day, and the app says to try again later. If the request itself fails, because the model refuses it or it comes back incomplete (a menu missing a day or a course is incomplete), it is given back a limited number of times: one for every ten recipes bought. After that such a failure counts, and the snackbar says how many recipes were taken. A request the ticked items can't satisfy at all is refused before anything is held. Retrying after a lost connection never takes a second one. The count on screen changes when the result appears.
